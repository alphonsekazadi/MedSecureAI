# Metformin (Glucophage)

**Auth0 FGA Metadata:**
- **Access Level:** `doctor, admin, pharmacist`
- **Prescribing Authority:** `doctor, nurse-practitioner`
- **Sensitivity:** `medium`
- **DEA Schedule:** `Non-controlled`
- **Last Updated:** `2025-10-12`

## Generic and Brand Names

- **Generic:** Metformin hydrochloride
- **Brand names:** Glucophage, Glucophage XR, Fortamet, Glumetza, Riomet

## Classification

- **Drug class:** Biguanide antidiabetic agent
- **Mechanism:** Decreases hepatic glucose production, improves insulin sensitivity

## Indications

### FDA-Approved Uses
- **Type 2 diabetes mellitus** (first-line therapy)
- **Pediatric type 2 diabetes** (≥10 years old)

### Off-Label Uses
- Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS)
- Weight management in obesity
- Prevention of diabetes in prediabetes
- Gestational diabetes (in some cases)

## Dosing and Administration

### Initial Dosing
- **Immediate-release:** 500 mg twice daily with meals
- **Extended-release:** 500-1000 mg once daily with evening meal
- **Oral solution:** 500 mg (5 mL) twice daily

### Dose Titration
- Increase by 500 mg weekly or 850 mg every 2 weeks
- **Maximum dose:** 2,550 mg/day (immediate-release), 2,000 mg/day (extended-release)
- Divide immediate-release doses with meals to reduce GI effects

### Renal Dosing
- **eGFR ≥45 mL/min/1.73m²:** No dose adjustment needed
- **eGFR 30-44 mL/min/1.73m²:** Consider dose reduction
- **eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73m²:** Contraindicated

## Contraindications

### Absolute Contraindications
- Severe kidney disease (eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73m²)
- Metabolic acidosis, including diabetic ketoacidosis
- Known hypersensitivity to metformin
- Acute or chronic conditions predisposing to lactic acidosis

### Relative Contraindications
- Liver disease or hepatic impairment
- Excessive alcohol use
- Advanced heart failure
- Recent MI or stroke
- Severe infection or dehydration

## Monitoring Parameters

### Baseline Assessment
- Serum creatinine and eGFR
- Liver function tests
- Complete blood count
- Vitamin B12 level (consider baseline)

### Ongoing Monitoring
- **Renal function:** Every 3-6 months initially, then annually
- **Vitamin B12:** Annually (long-term use)
- **HbA1c:** Every 3 months until stable, then every 6 months
- **Lactate:** If signs/symptoms of lactic acidosis

## Adverse Effects

### Common (>10%)
- Gastrointestinal upset (nausea, diarrhea, abdominal pain)
- Metallic taste
- Decreased appetite

### Less Common (1-10%)
- Vitamin B12 deficiency (long-term use)
- Lactic acidosis (rare but serious)

### Rare (<1%)
- Severe allergic reactions
- Megaloblastic anemia

## Drug Interactions

### Major Interactions
- **Iodinated contrast media:** Hold 48 hours before and after procedure
- **Carbonic anhydrase inhibitors:** Increased risk of lactic acidosis
- **Alcohol:** Increased risk of lactic acidosis

### Moderate Interactions
- **Furosemide:** May increase metformin levels
- **Nifedipine:** May increase metformin absorption
- **Cationic drugs:** Competition for renal elimination

## Clinical Considerations

### Pregnancy and Lactation
- **Pregnancy category B:** Generally considered safe
- **Lactation:** Small amounts excreted in breast milk
- Often preferred over insulin in gestational diabetes

### Surgical Procedures
- **Hold before contrast procedures** (48 hours)
- **Hold before major surgery** if risk of renal impairment
- **Resume when renal function stable**

### Patient Counseling Points
1. Take with meals to reduce stomach upset
2. Do not crush or chew extended-release tablets
3. Stay hydrated, especially during illness
4. Avoid excessive alcohol consumption
5. Report symptoms of lactic acidosis (weakness, muscle pain, breathing problems)
6. Continue healthy diet and exercise habits

## Efficacy Data

### Glycemic Control
- **HbA1c reduction:** 1.0-1.5% when used as monotherapy
- **Fasting glucose reduction:** 60-80 mg/dL
- **Weight neutral to modest weight loss:** 2-3 kg average

### Cardiovascular Benefits
- Reduced cardiovascular events in overweight patients (UKPDS)
- May have cardioprotective effects beyond glycemic control
- First-line choice in patients with cardiovascular disease
